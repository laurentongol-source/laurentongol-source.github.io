The STORY

The story is one of the most important aspects of your character. It defines what their traits, motivations, conflicts, and growth look like throughout the narrative and how it affects their development.

INTRODUCING YOUR CHARACTER

How your character is introduced in the story is one of the most important parts of adding characters into different types of stories. It gives your consumer the ability to be able the type of character they are about to be involved with and if they want to continue following along with the character. If your character is interesting off the bat, then the consumers are more likely to want to experience the rest of the story along with your character.

One of the basic ways of introducing your character is following these three simple steps when they are first introduced. The goal is to give the audience an immediate sense of who their character is and make them more easily attachable.

  1. Have your character SAY something ( Good starting dialogue )

    What they first say defines who they are as a character. What your character first says can introduce deduction from your audience telling your consumer something about your character right away.

  2. Have your character DO something

    What a character thinks and says is not the only thing that defines them as a character but their actions are what really show them off as a good character. It can show the character’s personality and what type of person they are.

  3. Show what the character’s PROBLEM is

    The majority of writers often put off giving the character a main problem or issue leaving it as an afterthought. Actually, it is more favourable when the problem is introduced in the first scene. Problems make characters feel human, characters without them are false characters. They don’t feel like real or relatable.

CHARACTER'S BACKSTORY AND EXPERIENCES

Characters always have interesting backstories for a reason. It’s not simple because it’s not just to add ‘trouble’ or ‘angst’ to a character. Making your character flawless with no issues makes them a flat character, especially if they’re one of your more important characters.

Before developing their backstory, it’s important to know who the character is in the current state of their story. What are their current defining personality, their negative and positive traits, and other basic information ( age, nationality/ethnicity, time period, etc ). Figuring out what your character is currently gives you a scaffold of what the backstory of your character would be and you can trace back to these if you’re feeling stuck during writing.

With the basics down, you can use those to understand what may have caused their upbringing. Their living conditions, their time period, and climate they have grown under may affect them in the long term. Having a word or small sentence describing the theme of their backstory can assist you in the creation of their story.

The backstory can be categorised into three segments which is similar to using a Plot Diagram when you're writing the actual overall storyline.

  1. Untouched

    The character before any major events of the backstory have happened ( pre - backstory )

  2. Event(s)

    The Event or pivotal moments that shape the character.

  3. Aftermath

    How the character comes out after the event. Showing how differently they act or think differently as of result of the event(s).

ⓘ Events change people in DIFFERENT ways, not every character reacts the same way as another. Just like in real life !
