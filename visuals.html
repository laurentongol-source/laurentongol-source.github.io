The VISUALS

As important as knowing the basics and story are, there is no character without the main design of the character. The visual design allows your audience to learn the character’s personality and lore just based on what they look like, dress like, and present as.

RESEARCH AND REFERENCE

Before you decide to initially start designing the appearance, one of the best starting points is knowing about what your design is based on or what type of vibe you want your character to bring out. Your character may be inspired by certain time periods or different cultures meaning you yourself would have to teach yourself how that culture addresses that type of attire. Even if your character is in a fantastical setting, you would still need references or guides to how you’re going to design clothing. The more you learn about that culture, time period, aesthetic, etc, the more you are able to integrate into not only your character’s appearance, but your character’s story and the overall worldbuilding.

When you’re figuring out aesthetics, creating mood boards are incredibly useful for figuring out the type of vibes your character should give off. Maybe they give off a more modern, cyber punk look? Or maybe you're returning to a more medieval aesthetic? However, you do not need to strictly follow every image or idea you’ve placed in your planning board! You easily use the mood board to inspire your character, your character can even look entirely different to the images on your board but just follow the feelings of the board.

Usual methods / websites to create moodboards on :

  • Pinterest

    One of the most popular websites for finding images/references. Entirely FREE, no payed limiations. It has a lot of imagery posted globally, there's pins for pretty much everything on there! However, contains a lot of AI imagery; be aware of what you save on your boards!

  • Milanote

    Another well known website, known for it's simple to grasp UI and Mechanics. Has a lot of features along with it's simple whiteboard features to layout your features. Be aware, there is a limit to how many items you place with your account! If you want increased space, you need to be ready to pay!

  • Traditionally

    Of course you don't have to only do your reference boards online only! You can easily make your own boards by using sticky notes and printed images on a pieace of card or a cork board. Although, this method is usually quite pricey and takes a lot more time than using digital methods.

IDEATION

Ideation is the stage in which you are planning what your character may look like. Obviously, your first design won’t look exactly like your final design. This is the part where you’re allowed to experiment, don’t be afraid to test new concepts you haven’t considered in earlier moments. It allows you to lay out all of your ideas, and break down all your concepts.

Thumbnail sketches are some of the easiest places to start if you don’t know where to start your ideas. These are sketches that are about the size of your thumb, intended to put down ideas quickly without putting excessive amounts of details or time onto unnecessary elements. From there, you can gradually increase the amount of designs you can create. Moving out of your comfort zones allows more intricate and more challenging designs. Create different silhouettes, linearts, thumbnails!

You might find that your first ‘final’ result won’t be exactly what you might’ve imagined. That is perfectly okay, it’s quite common for situations like this! When times like this happen, all you need to do is try again. Looking back at your old ideas, you might just find that your ideal design is hidden within those practice sketches you made in the earlier stages of the ideation process!

DEFINING YOUR CHARACTER'S IDENTITY

Characters are not only about how they act or what they say but how they are outside of your main plotline, how they interact with others, and how much they could change if the story were to switch paths.

Asking simple questions about your character can help you define what type of character you are looking for even if those questions won’t directly affect your character’s main plotline.

The importance of these simple development questions allows the character to feel less like just a character and more like like a relatable person. Just like in the real world, a character's hobbies, experiences, and influences define how they act as a person. If anything in their world were to change, how much would the character change? Would they stay the same? Or would they be a completely different person? The change in traits and personality gives the character a more human feel.

EXTRA TIPS TO KNOW

Don’t show EVERYTHING about a character’s backstory

A very easy trap writers fall into is where they explain every intricate detail about the character. The backstory is not only important for the character but for the overarching plot as well. Ask questions like : “How does this section of the backstory affect how they act in the main storyline?” You’ll mostly see younger perspectives of characters if they’re told through their perspective. However, you won’t see a lot of characters before the age you’ve met them because their story isn’t told from their perspective giving more open interpretation to how they could’ve ended up as they had

TRAUMA IS NOT YOUR MAIN ISSUE

A common misconception is that having too many bad events happen to your characters is unrealistic or tacky. However, the majority of the time many bad things will happen in succession as a result of another event. Take example, ( if you’re writing a story based on the real-world ) ignoring major historical events can be seen as ignorant, your characters would also be affected by these occurrences. The issue isn’t the amount of events. It’s how none of the events have common ground, feeling pointless to the backstory. Make your backstory consistent, link back to your main theme of your character’s past. (almost) Every varying event will follow the overarching wound or theme according to the character.

WRITE WHAT YOU KNOW

This tip does not mean only write what you know. By expanding your knowledge on other topics, it allows you as the writer to learn new things. However, this point is to include details from your own life along with the mix of newly researched ideas it gives your character a type of harmony and balance.

If you are writing about a topic you don’t have that much knowledge about, just simply do some research! ( a lot of research ) Research from different sources - whether it be first or secondhand knowledge - because in the end your story is inspired by people and the real world. (fictional or not)
