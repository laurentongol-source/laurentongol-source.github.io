WHAT IS A CHARACTER

Whenever people mention writing, characters are pretty much guaranteed to pop up in the conversation. Characters are used in almost all forms of narrative writing to convey the emotions of a story. Characters are fictional humans, animals, or others portrayed by actors, visual artists, and authors written for things such as movies , books, plays, games etc.

They are your key if you want a more relatable or emotional plot to your overarching stories. Majority of the time, having a set of likeable and dislikable characters in your story’s cast keeps your reader invested in your storyline. Wanting to watch your characters grow, develop, and work keeps your audience focused on the story and continues to consume more of the story / media.

Characters are one of the most important aspects of any narrative based story, whether it be first person, second or third. They are known to be the heart of the story, the reasons why viewers get so attached to shows, movies, books, games, and other forms of media.

They allow your plot to push forward. Your character’s actions will matter to the story, their actions will add to your story in substantial ways. Because they have their own subplots and personalities, they drive the story forward in their very own way without the plot feeling random.

Humans are all emotional in some way, some greater than others. Which is why characters tend to have their own values and morals, giving them their own emotions. Characters bring emotion to your storyline, their relatableness and struggles allows the audience to care and want to continue the story.

Also, characters can present different morals, themes, and lessons through their actions alone. It’s easier for readers to take in because it’s a character they presumably enjoy. Through their journey and arcs, they’re most likely to explore concepts, moral changes, personal changes that humans normally go through making it easier for the audience to digest

TYPES OF CHARACTERS

In most stories, you won’t see just one character throughout the story. It usually comes off as bland and boring if your stories are about one character and that character only. Creating your cast for the story allows your readers to fully grasp how your character acts and responds with other characters. Those other characters are usually the key for your central character to progress with their newfound relationships. ( Good or bad ! )

These are the basic characters you'll see in majority stories consisting of characters

  • Untouched

    The character before any major events of the backstory have happened ( pre - backstory )

  • Event(s)

    The Event or pivotal moments that shape the character.

  • Aftermath

    How the character comes out after the event. Showing how differently they act or think differently as of result of the event(s).
